Shely's Poster

3RS-SR: Virtual Patient Validation Study

Shely Khaikin¹, Lauren Lipner², Ronit Shvarzman³, Bernard Gorman⁴, J.C. Muran⁴, Catherine F. Eubanks⁴, & Sarah Bloch-Elkouby¹˒³

¹ Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai
² Fairleigh Dickinson University, USA
³ Yeshiva University
⁴ Adelphi University, New York, USA

Background

The therapeutic alliance has repeatedly been identified as a robust predictor of psychotherapy outcomes and retention (Flückiger et al., 2018). Alliance ruptures, or breakdowns in the collaboration or agreement of psychotherapy tasks and goals or in the affective bond, are common in psychotherapy and predict poorer outcomes when unrepaired (Eubanks et al., 2018).

Although the Rupture Resolution Rating Scale (3RS) is a well-validated observer-based measure used to identify patient rupture markers and therapist repair strategies within psychotherapy sessions (Eubanks et al., 2015), the self-report version (3RS-SR) has yet to be validated in the general population, let alone with a high-risk patient population.

Study Aims

The aim of this study was to take advantage of clinicians’ interactions with a standardized virtual patient at high risk for suicide to assess the psychometric properties of the 3RS-SR instrument.

Method

Seventy psychologists completed the 3RS-SR based on their clinical interactions with a virtual adolescent patient. The study assessed the factor structure, internal consistency, interrater reliability, and convergent validity of the measure.

Analytic Approach:

  • Principal component analysis to assess factor structure.
  • Cronbach’s alpha to assess internal consistency.
  • McDonald’s omega to assess reliability.
  • (r_{wg}) to assess interrater reliability.
  • Pearson’s (r) to assess convergent validity.
Study characteristic Description
Sample size 70 psychologists
Study design Virtual patient validation study
Clinical context Clinician interactions with a virtual adolescent patient at high risk for suicide
