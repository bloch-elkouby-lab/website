Hanjiang’s Poster

Assessing Psychometric Properties of Suicide Crisis Syndrome Checklist (SCS-C) Using a Standardized Virtual Patient

Hanjiang Xu¹, Jimmy Chen¹˒³, Violet Yang¹˒³, Sarah Bloch-Elkouby²˒³

¹ Teachers College, Columbia University
² Ferkauf Graduate School of Psychology, Yeshiva University
³ Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai

Background:

The Suicide Crisis Syndrome (SCS) is a newly developed acute state indicating an imminent risk for suicidal behaviors (Schuck et al., 2019; Bloch-Elkouby et al., 2024). The SCS contains five main components:

  • A: Entrapment
  • B1: Affective disturbance
  • B2: Loss of cognitive control
  • B3: Disturbances in arousal
  • C: Social withdrawal

The SCS may provide clinicians with the ability to identify individuals experiencing an acute pre-suicidal mental state, regardless of their self-reported suicidal ideation.

Study Aims:

The original Suicide Crisis Syndrome Checklist (SCS-C) relies on a restrictive, dichotomous (Yes/No) scale (Bloch-Elkouby et al., 2026). Preliminary research suggests that transitioning to a continuous scale may improve the tool’s sensitivity, variance, and overall psychometric properties.

The present study aims to evaluate the factor structure and goodness-of-fit of the continuous SCS-C, specifically comparing a one-factor model and a five-factor model, using a standardized virtual patient.

Method:

The study used a standardized virtual patient to assess the psychometric properties of the continuous Suicide Crisis Syndrome Checklist (SCS-C).

The poster identifies the following five-factor structure:

  1. Entrapment
  2. Affective disturbance
  3. Loss of cognitive control
  4. Disturbances in arousal
  5. Social withdrawal

Results:

The five-factor macro model succeeded.

The chi-square statistic was not significant, indicating that the hypothesized model did not significantly differ from the observed data. The Comparative Fit Index exceeded the conventional criterion of .95, indicating excellent model fit. The Tucker-Lewis Index exceeded the commonly accepted cutoff of .90, indicating acceptable fit. The Standardized Root Mean Square Residual fell below the recommended threshold of .08, indicating minimal discrepancies between observed and predicted correlations.

Model fit statistic Result Interpretation
Chi-square χ² = 8.28, p = .14 Non-significant; hypothesized model did not significantly differ from observed data
Comparative Fit Index (CFI) .955 Excellent model fit
Tucker-Lewis Index (TLI) .909 Acceptable fit
Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR) .048 Below recommended threshold of .08

Factor Loadings

Factor loadings indicate the degree to which each domain reflects the underlying Suicide Crisis Syndrome construct. The poster reports that all factor loadings were statistically significant and ranged from moderate to strong magnitude.

Internal Consistency Reliability

The poster reports a Cronbach’s alpha of .71, indicating acceptable baseline scale dependability. The remaining reliability statistics are not visible in the provided screenshot.

Collectively, the findings support the theoretical conceptualization of Suicide Crisis Syndrome as a multidimensional construct consisting of five related symptom domains.

Discussion:

The findings support the five-factor structure of the continuous SCS-C and provide evidence for its psychometric properties using a standardized virtual patient. The results support the theoretical conceptualization of Suicide Crisis Syndrome as a multidimensional construct composed of five related symptom domains.

The continuous version of the SCS-C may offer improvements over the original dichotomous Yes/No format by increasing sensitivity, variance, and overall psychometric properties.
