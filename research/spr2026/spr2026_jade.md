# Does Mindfulness Moderate the Impact of Patients’ Race and Clinicians’ Implicit Racial Biases on Clinicians’ Emotional Response to Minoritized Youth?

## Background

Provider bias may contribute to persistent disparities in mental health service utilization. The impact of provider bias on clinicians’ emotional responses in therapy is not well understood. The potential protective role of clinicians’ mindfulness in this context has not been explored.

This study examines:

1. How patients’ race and clinicians’ implicit racial bias affect clinicians’ emotional responses.
2. Whether clinicians’ mindfulness moderates these effects.

## Hypotheses

1. Clinicians would show significantly different emotional responses to virtual patients of different races.
2. There would be an interaction effect between virtual patients’ race and clinicians’ implicit racial biases on clinicians’ emotional responses, such that clinicians with higher implicit racial bias would report more negative emotional responses to Black versus White virtual patients.
3. Clinicians’ mindfulness would moderate the relationship between virtual patients’ race, clinicians’ implicit racial biases, and clinicians’ emotional responses. Specifically, among clinicians with higher mindfulness, the relationship between patient race and implicit racial bias and emotional response would be weaker.

## Method

**Participants:** 200 mental health professionals.

**Design:** Participants were randomly assigned to interact with virtual patients of different races (Black vs. White).

**Procedure:** Clinicians conducted simulated clinical interviews with virtual patients.

**Measures:**

1. **Emotional response:** Therapist Response Questionnaire–Short Form (TRQ-SF).
2. **Mindfulness:** Mindful Attention Awareness Scale (MAAS).
3. **Implicit bias:** Implicit Association Test (IAT).

**Statistical Analysis:** Independent-samples t-test and PROCESS macro (Models 1 and 3) were used to test moderation and three-way interaction effects.

| Study characteristic | Description |
|---|---|
| Participants | 200 mental health professionals |
| Study design | Randomized virtual patient study |
| Patient race conditions | Black vs. White virtual patients |
| Emotional response measure | TRQ-SF |
| Mindfulness measure | MAAS |
| Implicit bias measure | IAT |
| Statistical analyses | Independent-samples t-test and PROCESS macro (Models 1 and 3) |

## Results

### 1. Race Differences (RQ1)

There was no significant difference in clinicians’ emotional responses between those assigned to White and Black/Other virtual patients.

| Virtual patient condition | Mean | SD | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| White | 17.19 | 4.74 | 100 |
| Black/Other | 18.21 | 4.91 | 91 |

\(t(185.9) = -1.46,\ p = .147\)

95% CI [−2.40, 0.36]
