## Background

Therapeutic alliance is a key component of effective psychotherapy and treatment outcomes. Previous research has examined the relationships between empathy, mindfulness, and therapeutic alliance. Empathy and mindfulness have been shown to enhance relational attunement, strengthen therapist presence, and facilitate rupture repair.

However, empathy and mindfulness have not been studied as moderators of implicit bias effects on therapeutic alliance.

## Current Study

The present study addresses this gap by examining empathy and mindfulness as potential moderators in the relationship between implicit bias and therapeutic alliance.

## Hypotheses

1. **H1:** Therapist empathy will moderate the relationship between implicit bias and therapeutic alliance.
2. **H2:** Therapist mindfulness will moderate the relationship between implicit bias and therapeutic alliance.

## Method

**Participants:** Mental health professionals participating in a larger study examining relationships between racial bias, clinical judgment, and suicide risk assessment using virtual patients.

**Design:** Clinicians interacted with one of two versions of Noah, a Black or White virtual patient, followed by a battery of questionnaires about their interaction.

**Measures:**

1. **Implicit Bias:** Implicit Association Test (IAT_d).
2. **Empathy:** Therapist Empathy Scale (TES; α = .98), observer-coded.
3. **Mindfulness:** Mindful Attention Awareness Scale (MAAS; α = .86).
4. **Therapeutic Alliance:** 3RS Working Together subscale (α = .84).

**Statistical Analysis:** Preliminary correlational analyses (Pearson's r) examined relationships among mindfulness, empathy, implicit bias, and therapeutic alliance. Moderation analyses were conducted using the PROCESS macro (Models 1 and 2) to examine the individual and combined moderating effects of empathy and mindfulness.

| Study characteristic | Description |
|---|---|
| Study context | Larger study examining racial bias, clinical judgment, and suicide risk assessment |
| Virtual patient conditions | Black vs. White virtual patients (Noah) |
| Implicit bias measure | Implicit Association Test (IAT_d) |
| Empathy measure | Therapist Empathy Scale (TES; α = .98) |
| Mindfulness measure | Mindful Attention Awareness Scale (MAAS; α = .86) |
| Alliance measure | 3RS Working Together subscale (α = .84) |
| Statistical analyses | Pearson correlations and PROCESS moderation analyses (Models 1 and 2) |

## Results

### 1. Preliminary Correlational Analyses

Preliminary correlational analyses revealed:

- Mindfulness was significantly positively correlated with therapeutic alliance (r = .16, p = .049).
- Empathy was positively but not significantly correlated with therapeutic alliance (r = .14, p = .10).
- Implicit bias was negatively but not significantly correlated with therapeutic alliance (r = −.13, p = .12).
- Empathy and mindfulness were weakly correlated (r = .12, p = .19).

### 2. Moderation Analyses

**Model 1: Empathy as Moderator**

The overall model was not significant, R² = .04, F(3,116) = 1.49, p = .22.

Neither implicit bias (b = −.40, p = .13), empathy (b = .16, p = .14), nor their interaction (b = .19, p = .39) significantly predicted therapeutic alliance.

**Model 1: Mindfulness as Moderator**

The model approached significance, R² = .05, F(3,140) = 2.37, p = .07.

- Implicit bias negatively predicted therapeutic alliance at trend level (b = −.43, p = .07).
- Mindfulness positively predicted therapeutic alliance at trend level (b = .30, p = .06).
- The interaction term was not significant (b = .43, p = .28).

**Model 2: Empathy and Mindfulness Combined**

The combined model was not significant, R² = .05, F(5,114) = 1.29, p = .27. Neither empathy nor mindfulness significantly moderated the relationship between implicit bias and therapeutic alliance.

### 3. Conditional Effects

Conditional effects probing suggested that implicit bias predicted lower therapeutic alliance particularly at low levels of mindfulness, whereas at high mindfulness, the bias–alliance relationship was attenuated.

However, these trends were not statistically significant.

## Discussion

The present study tested whether therapist empathy and mindfulness moderated the impact of implicit bias on therapeutic alliance.

Contrary to what was hypothesized, neither empathy nor mindfulness significantly buffered the relationship between implicit bias and therapeutic alliance.

However, mindfulness showed a consistent, trend-level positive association with therapeutic alliance, suggesting its contribution may be more direct than interactive.

The findings highlight mindfulness as a relational resource that may enhance therapist presence and attunement, while underscoring the complexity of implicit bias and the limitations of self-report empathy measures.

## Limitations

1. **Preliminary findings:** Empathy coding was still in progress at the time of poster submission. Findings may shift once coding is complete.
2. **Simulated interactions:** Virtual patient interactions cannot fully capture the complexity and nuance of real clinical encounters.
3. **Self-report measures:** Reliance on self-report measures for mindfulness and therapeutic alliance introduces the possibility of social desirability effects.
4. **Sample size:** The relatively small sample limits statistical power and generalizability.
